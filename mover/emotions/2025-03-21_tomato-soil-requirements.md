Tomatoes thrive in **well-draining, nutrient-rich, slightly acidic soil** with a pH between **6.0 and 6.8**. The ideal soil composition includes:  

### **1. Soil Type:**  
- **Loamy soil** is best, as it retains moisture while allowing excess water to drain.  
- **Sandy loam** is also good if amended with compost to hold nutrients.  

### **2. Nutrients & Amendments:**  
- **Organic matter**: Mix in compost, aged manure, or worm castings to improve fertility.  
- **Balanced fertilizer**: Tomatoes need **nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K)**, but too much nitrogen can lead to excessive leaves and fewer fruits. A **5-10-10 or 10-10-10** fertilizer works well.  
- **Calcium source**: Add **bone meal, crushed eggshells, or gypsum** to prevent blossom end rot.  
- **Mulch**: Apply straw, grass clippings, or wood chips to retain moisture and regulate soil temperature.  

### **3. Drainage & Aeration:**  
- Ensure good drainage to **prevent root rot**. If soil is heavy clay, amend it with **sand, compost, or perlite** for aeration.  
- Raised beds or containers work well if your soil is too dense.

For a **raised bed tomato soil mix**, you want a blend that retains moisture, drains well, and provides long-lasting nutrients. Here’s a **high-performing soil mix recipe** for tomatoes:

### **Best Soil Mix for Raised Beds (per 4x4 ft bed, 12 inches deep)**
#### **Base Mix (60%)**
- **1 part topsoil** (or screened native soil if available)
- **1 part compost** (aged manure, mushroom compost, or homemade compost)
- **1 part aeration material** (coarse sand, perlite, or pumice)

#### **Nutrient Boosters (40%)**
- **1-2 cups bone meal or rock phosphate** (for phosphorus, root growth, and flowering)
- **1-2 cups blood meal or feather meal** (for nitrogen, leafy growth)
- **1-2 cups wood ash or potassium sulfate** (for potassium, disease resistance, and fruiting)
- **2-3 cups garden lime or crushed eggshells** (for calcium, preventing blossom end rot)
- **1-2 cups worm castings or alfalfa meal** (boosts microbial life and nutrients)

#### **pH Balancers (if needed)**
- **Limestone or garden lime** (if pH is below 6.0)
- **Sulfur or peat moss** (if pH is above 6.8)

#### **Mulch (Top Layer)**
- **Straw, grass clippings, or wood chips** to retain moisture and regulate temperature.

### **Optional Additions**
- **Mycorrhizal fungi** (boosts root growth and nutrient absorption)
- **Biochar** (enhances soil structure and microbial activity)
- **A handful of Epsom salt per bed** (for magnesium, improving fruit set)

### **Maintenance Tips**
- **Before planting:** Water the soil thoroughly and let it settle for a day or two.
- **During the season:** Top-dress with compost and side-dress with organic fertilizer every few weeks.
- **Crop rotation:** Avoid planting tomatoes in the same bed every year to prevent soil-borne diseases.

### **Custom Tomato Fertilization Schedule**
#### **Before Planting (Bed Preparation)**
- **2-3 weeks before planting:**  
  - Mix in **compost (2-3 inches deep)** to enrich the soil.  
  - Add **bone meal (2 cups per 4x4 bed)** for phosphorus.  
  - Add **blood meal or feather meal (2 cups per 4x4 bed)** for nitrogen.  
  - Add **wood ash or potassium sulfate (1-2 cups per 4x4 bed)** for potassium.  
  - Add **garden lime or crushed eggshells (2-3 cups per 4x4 bed)** for calcium.  
  - Water the bed thoroughly and let it settle.

---

#### **Planting Day**
- Sprinkle **mycorrhizal fungi** directly on the roots to enhance nutrient uptake.
- Add **a handful of Epsom salt per plant** (magnesium for strong cell walls).
- Mulch the soil with **straw, wood chips, or grass clippings** to retain moisture.

---

### **Growth Stages & Feeding Schedule**

#### **1. Early Growth (Weeks 1-3)**
- **After transplanting**, wait **1-2 weeks** before feeding to allow roots to establish.
- Apply a **balanced organic fertilizer (5-10-10 or 10-10-10)** every **10-14 days**.
  - **Alternative organic feed:**  
    - **Compost tea or fish emulsion (diluted as per instructions)**
    - **Liquid seaweed extract (optional, boosts root development)**  

---

#### **2. Pre-Flowering (Weeks 4-6)**
- **Reduce nitrogen to focus on flowering and fruit set.**
- **Side-dress** with:
  - **1 cup bone meal per plant** (phosphorus boost)
  - **1 cup wood ash per plant** (potassium boost)
- Apply **Epsom salt spray (1 tbsp per gallon of water)** once every **2 weeks** to prevent magnesium deficiency.
- Continue watering deeply **2-3 times per week**.

---

#### **3. Flowering & Early Fruit Set (Weeks 7-10)**
- **Boost potassium and phosphorus for strong fruit production.**
- Feed with:
  - **Liquid kelp extract or compost tea every 10-14 days**.
  - **Side-dress plants with ½ cup bone meal & ½ cup potassium sulfate per plant.**
  - Continue **Epsom salt spray** to enhance fruit set.

---

#### **4. Peak Fruit Production (Weeks 11-15)**
- Tomatoes are heavy feeders now!  
- Feed every **7-10 days** with:
  - **Organic tomato fertilizer (low nitrogen, high phosphorus & potassium)**
  - **Banana peel tea** (potassium boost)
  - **Liquid kelp extract or fish emulsion** for micronutrients.
- **Stop adding nitrogen** unless plants look pale or yellow.

---

#### **5. Late Season (Weeks 16+ or End of Harvest)**
- Reduce feeding but continue **compost tea or seaweed extract every 2 weeks**.
- Remove older leaves to improve air circulation.
- Once temperatures cool, **reduce watering** to prevent splitting fruit.

---

### **Bonus Pro Tips**
✅ **Mulch regularly** to retain moisture and prevent soil-borne diseases.  
✅ **Prune suckers** (small shoots between the main stem and branches) to focus energy on fruiting.  
✅ **Rotate crops yearly**—avoid planting tomatoes in the same bed to prevent disease buildup.
