# Slurs and Dynamics Explained

What are the arching lines mean

**Assistant** (2025-05-17T01:08:47.602693):
The arching lines you see in the sheet music are **slurs**. A **slur** is a curved line that connects two or more different notes, indicating that they should be played **smoothly and connected** — this is called **legato** playing.

Here’s the breakdown:
- **Slurs (arching lines)** = play the notes smoothly without separation.
- They're not to be confused with **ties**, which look similar but only connect **two notes of the same pitch**, combining their lengths.

In your image:
- These slurs tell the musician to keep the notes flowing together within the arch rather than breaking them up with tonguing (for wind instruments) or separation (for strings or voice).

Also, shoutout to the markings — “mp” for mezzo piano (medium soft), “f” for forte (loud), and “getting louder” — likely referring to a **crescendo** coming up. Love the annotations; they show someone’s actively engaging with the dynamics!
