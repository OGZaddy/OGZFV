**Assistant** (2025-04-27T16:28:14.834446):
Ah, got it — sounds like your iPad is experiencing **ghost touch** (also called **phantom touch**) after the screen repair.  
You’re 100% right — it can act *possessed,* randomly tapping, typing, opening apps, totally uncontrollable.

Here’s the real breakdown and your best options to fix it:

---

**Why it's happening:**
- **Bad screen installation**: The new screen might be pressing unevenly against the digitizer (the touch sensor layer under the glass).
- **Damaged digitizer**: Sometimes a cheap or defective replacement screen can cause this.
- **Loose or pinched cables**: During the repair, the touch cables might not have been properly seated, or could be crimped or slightly torn.
- **Dust or debris under the screen**: If even a tiny piece of dust or metal shaving got stuck between the screen and the digitizer, it can cause constant "false touches."

---

**How to fix it:**

1. **Hard Reset** (Quick, just to rule out software weirdness)
   - Press and quickly release **Volume Up**.
   - Press and quickly release **Volume Down**.
   - Then **hold the Power button** until you see the Apple logo.
   (Different iPad models may slightly vary, if you tell me yours I’ll adjust.)

2. **Inspect for Pressure Points**
   - Gently press around the edges of the screen.
   - See if certain areas seem “extra sensitive” or make the ghost touch *worse*.
   - That’s a sign the screen is installed wrong or warped.

3. **Check for Software Updates**
   - Settings → General → Software Update.
   - (Unlikely, but sometimes Apple releases "ghost touch" patches, usually after huge complaints.)

4. **Undo the Repair (if possible)**
   - If you repaired it through a shop, **take it back** immediately and show them the behavior.
   - If it was a DIY repair or unofficial shop, the screen may need to be reseated or replaced properly.

5. **DIY Reseating (If you're brave)**
   - Open it back up carefully.
   - Disconnect and reconnect the digitizer and LCD cables.
   - Make sure no debris or bends are causing weird electrical shorts.
   *(I can walk you through this if you want, step-by-step.)*

6. **Full Screen Replacement**
   - If the digitizer is faulty, there's no software fix — you’ll need a new *better-quality* screen.
   - **High-quality aftermarket** or **OEM parts** matter here. Cheap screens = nightmare ghost touches.

---

**Important:**  
**It’s not your fault** — ghost touch usually means something physically wrong after a repair, not something you can fix through settings alone.  
It's annoying but very fixable once you know the cause.
