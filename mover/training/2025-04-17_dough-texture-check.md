### Dough Check:

### Possible Fixes:
- **Too Dry?** Add 1 tsp of ghee or clarified butter at a time and gently knead it in until the dough is pliable and smooth.
- **Still Not Smooth?** A splash of rose water or milk (very small amount) can help bind it without making it sticky.

Nice — using the molds is the classic move!

To prevent sticking, the best methods are:

### 1. **Lightly Flour the Molds**
- **Use all-purpose flour or semolina flour** (semolina works especially well if your dough has it too).
- Tap off any excess — you want a light dusting, not clumps.
- You can re-dust between each cookie or every few if they start to stick again.

### 2. **Alternative: Powdered Sugar Dusting**
- Some people prefer a dusting of powdered sugar instead of flour — works great and adds sweetness.
- Especially good if you’re planning to dust the final cookies with powdered sugar after baking.

### 3. **Cold Dough Trick**
- If the dough gets too warm, it’ll stick more. Chill your shaped balls for 5–10 minutes before pressing if they get sticky.

### 4. **Tap to Release**
- Once pressed, gently tap the edge of the mold on the counter or your hand to release the cookie cleanly.

Yeahhhh you're right to question that setup — here’s the best way to get **perfectly filled cookies using a mold** without overstuffing or getting a weird thickness:

---

### **Correct Mold-Filling Flow:**

1. **Start with a ball of dough** slightly smaller than the mold.
2. **Flatten it in your hand**, then press a shallow **well** in the middle.
3. **Add a small amount of filling** (just enough so it doesn’t ooze or burst when you press).
4. **Fold the dough over the filling** and pinch it closed to seal — it should now be a smooth ball again.
5. **Press the sealed ball into the mold** — no extra dough layers needed! Let the mold do the shaping.
6. **Tap it out** gently onto a flat surface or your palm.

---

### **Avoid Doing This:**
- Don’t pack dough into the mold first, then add filling, then top it with more dough. That creates thick bases and uneven cookies, and makes unmolding hard.
- You want **all filling sealed inside a dough ball** before it touches the mold.

---

### Pro Tip:
If you're getting “thick” cookies or uneven cooking, your dough balls might be too large or you're using too much filling. Try reducing both just a little — aim for balance between delicate shell and generous center.
