Yeah bro — **the blade and the guard both make a *huge* difference**, but based on what you’re describing (pinching, dull feel, not cutting clean), this is *not* about the guard. That sounds like either:

---

### 🔧 1. **Misaligned or Unset Blade**
Your **BabyBliss Pro trimmer blade is likely out of alignment or zero-gapped wrong** from the factory. Common with new units. If the **moving blade isn’t flush** with the stationary one, it’ll **pinch, tug, and not cut cleanly** — especially on close fades or beards.

**Fix**:
- Grab a small screwdriver.
- Look at the blade straight on.
- Make sure the **moving blade (the bottom one)** is **not longer** than the top. It should be **even or just barely offset**.
- If needed: loosen the screws, adjust slightly, hold tight, then re-tighten.
- Test on your hand or arm before your face — see if it cuts without snag.

---

### 🔪 2. **Wrong Blade Type or Coating**
Some versions of the **FX trimmers come with a fade blade or deep-tooth blade** — not ideal for everyone. If you got a version with a **deep tooth "360° exposed" blade**, it can feel snaggy unless zero-gapped perfectly.

**Alternative**:
- Consider swapping to the **Graphite 2.0 blade** or **Gold FX DLC** if you want a smoother, skin-friendly cut.
- These are **much sharper, less grabby, and don’t burn or pinch**.

---

### 🧼 3. **Factory Gunk**
It’s common for new trimmers to have **factory oil or wax** on the blade. That can gunk it up and feel dull.

**Fix**:
- Spray it with **Andis Cool Care**, **Barbicide**, or even **91% isopropyl alcohol**.
- Run it for 10–15 seconds and wipe down.
- Re-oil with a **drop of Wahl or BaByliss clipper oil**.

---

### 🧢 4. **Guard Can Matter, But It Ain’t the Main Issue Here**
The **guard only controls the cut length**, not the *sharpness or feel*. Cheap plastic guards can vibrate or pop off, but if you're *pinching*, it's not the guard — it’s the **blade’s edge and spacing**.

---

Your $200 trimmer should feel **crisp**, not **snaggy or dull**. If it **pinches**, the **blade is likely misaligned or needs zero-gapping and cleaning**. You’re not crazy — it’s a common problem even with high-end trimmers out of the box.

Absolutely — this is barber gold right here. Fading **scissor length down to a #2**, and **blending 0 to 2**, is all about **control, transition, and layering** — whether you're doing it on your own head, someone else's, or trying to clean up a cut.

---

## 🎯 GOAL 1: **Fade From Scissor Length to a #2**
(This is often the **top into sides** blend)

### 🔧 Tools You Need:
- Clippers with guards (#2, #1.5, #1, #0.5)
- Taper lever (essential!)
- Shears or texturizing scissors (optional)
- Comb (wide and tight tooth)
- Trimmer (for lining up the bottom)

---

### ✂️ **Step-by-Step: Scissor Length to a #2 Fade**

1. **Start with the Top**
   - Trim or texturize to the desired “scissor length” (usually 3–5 inches).
   - Use point cutting if you want a textured look.

2. **Create Your Guideline at the Parietal Ridge**
   - This is the point where the top curves down to the sides.
   - Use your **#2 guard**, and run it **straight up and off the head** to avoid creating a shelf.

3. **Begin Fading Down**
   - Go from **#2** down to **#1.5**, then **#1**, then **open taper (0.5)** as you go lower.
   - Use a **C-shape flicking motion** at each level to blend.
   - Always blend **a little above** where your last line was.

4. **Scissor Over Comb or Clipper Over Comb**
   - At the ridge, **blend the longer scissor-cut hair into the clipper fade** using:
     - Scissor over comb for finesse.
     - Clipper over comb for speed.

5. **Use Detail Blending**
   - If there’s a visible line between scissor and clipper sections, go back with:
     - **#1.5 or #1 guard**
     - **Clipper open (lever up)**
     - Light corner blending motion

---

## 🎯 GOAL 2: **Fade From Skin (0) Up to a #2**
(This is the **bottom part of your fade**)

### 🧰 Tools You Need:
- Trimmer or foil shaver (for the “0” skin level)
- Clipper with taper lever + guards

---

### 🧼 Step-by-Step: 0 to 2 Fade

1. **Bald the Bottom (Skin Line)**
   - Use trimmers or a foil shaver up to the desired first guideline (usually **around the ear or mid-fade line**).
   - Keep the line **consistent and even**.

2. **Set Guideline #2 – 0.5 (Lever Open)**
   - About 0.5–1" above the skin line
   - Use the clipper with **no guard, lever open**
   - This is your transition zone between bald and #1

3. **Blend Between 0 and 0.5**
   - Close the lever halfway → blend from skin to 0.5
   - Use corner of blade and a flicking motion
   - Repeat with **lever fully closed** for tightest blend

4. **Apply #1 Guard (Lever Open)**
   - Go 1/2" above the previous guide
   - Blend upward using a light flick
   - Then close the lever and blend the bottom of this zone

5. **Apply #1.5 Guard**
   - Repeat flick-up motion — this will start the blend into the #2
   - Use your eye — fade into the #2 guideline you already cut above

6. **Final Step: Re-run the #2 to Clean It**
   - Make sure it’s even and clean all around
   - Optional: trim the crown or occipital area to avoid weight buildup

---

## 💡 Pro Tips:

- **Fade wet hair with scissors**, dry hair with clippers (so you see true bulk)
- **Dark hair shows lines more**, so blend carefully
- **Always blend “up” into the next length**, never start at the top of the fade and go down
- Use **corners of the blade** for tough spots or blend lines

---

## 🧠 Cheat Sheet Summary:

| Area         | Guard/Length | Method                         |
|--------------|--------------|--------------------------------|
| Top (scissor length) | 3–5"+        | Scissors, point cutting      |
| Parietal Ridge → #2 | #2 guard      | Clip up & flick off ridge    |
| Mid Fade         | #1.5 → #1    | Flick blend, clipper over comb |
| Low Fade (to skin) | 0.5 open → 0 closed | Precision flick, corner blend |
