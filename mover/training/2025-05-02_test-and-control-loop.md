## Top Outdoor Spots for Shooting & Campfires

### 1. Cross Bar SRMA – Amarillo, TX (Panhandle Plains)  
This **11,883-acre** public area managed by the BLM is a hidden gem of the Texas Panhandle【24†L359-L367】. It features **rugged canyons and open shortgrass prairie**, giving off serious wide-open *“middle of nowhere”* vibes. **Target shooting and hunting are permitted** here — in fact, it's the *only* BLM land in Texas where you can freely shoot【8†L280-L288】. Camping is **primitive and free**: no services at all, so bring everything you need and pack out all trash【8†L296-L304】. Campfires are allowed **when no burn ban is in effect** (always double-check local restrictions), and you'll want to keep fires small and controlled given the dry prairie environment. Privacy is practically guaranteed – the Cross Bar sees few visitors due to its remote location. *Note:* Access can be tricky; the land is land-locked by private ranches and the Canadian River, and reaching it requires driving through a riverbed with a high-clearance or 4x4 vehicle【26†L39-L47】. There are **no fees or permits** required to use this area, but be prepared for a true off-grid adventure.

### 2. LBJ National Grasslands – Decatur, TX (North Texas)  
If you’re after **Texas prairie and “cornfield” vibes** closer to the Dallas/Fort Worth area, the LBJ Grasslands might fit the bill. This 20,000-acre expanse of grassland and woodlands offers **dispersed camping and a few developed campgrounds**. Campfires are permitted in fire rings (and in dispersed sites with caution) so long as there’s no county burn ban. **Recreational shooting here is limited** – by Forest Service rules, *target shooting is only allowed with shotguns* in a designated area (Unit 3) of the grasslands【9†L339-L347】. That means you can enjoy some clay pigeon shooting or skeet, but rifle/pistol plinking is generally *not* allowed on this public land (outside of lawful hunting seasons). There are no entrance fees or permits needed for general use, but if you plan to hunt you’ll need a license. The Grasslands are moderately popular for hiking, horseback riding, and fishing, but with its many acres, you can still find a **secluded spot** to set up camp. The vibe is rustic and peaceful – think **open fields dotted with oak groves**, big Texas skies, and the occasional distant moo from cattle on neighboring properties.

### 3. Davy Crockett National Forest – East Texas  
For a more wooded setting, Davy Crockett National Forest offers **160,000+ acres of East Texas piney woods** with some clearings and backroads perfect for a private retreat. Dispersed camping is allowed (or use designated sites like primitive hunter camps), and you can have a campfire **if conditions allow** (always check for burn bans or Forest Service fire restrictions【21†L1-L8】). The forest allows **target shooting in the general forest areas** as long as you follow safety rules (good backstop, away from roads/trails)【12†L499-L507】. *Important:* Parts of this forest are Wildlife Management Areas (WMA) where target practice is **prohibited**【12†L503-L510】, so stick to the non-WMA portions (no special permit required for those). There are **no fees** to camp or shoot on general National Forest land here. Expect a moderate level of privacy – the forest is vast, and if you venture down remote dirt roads you can find a clearing all to yourself. The vibe here is **quiet and natural**: tall pines above, soft needles underfoot, and perhaps a creek nearby. It’s an ideal spot to roast some marshmallows, do a little plinking, and enjoy the sounds of an Eastern fox squirrel or two for company.

Small campfires are a go – the ranch provides **fire pits by each cabin** (and often firewood too) so you can safely have your campfire under the stars. Because it’s a private Airbnb-style rental, **you'll need to book a cabin or the ranch house**; rates will apply (typically per night). No permits needed since it’s private land. Privacy level is excellent: when you book, it’s essentially **your group on the ranch’s acres** with no random public around【36†L105-L113】. The vibe is the best of both worlds – **scenic and secluded**, yet with comfy amenities. Picture a classic East Texas landscape of **rolling pastures, wooded trails, and a fishing lake**, where you can spend the day shooting, riding ATVs, or even axe-throwing, then unwind around a crackling fire at night. *(Bonus:)* The ranch is known for its friendly hosts and even has hot tubs and a pool if you fancy a dip after a long day outdoors!

### 5. Selah Springs Ranch – Brady, TX (Private Hill Country Retreat)  
Selah Springs is a **1,000-acre ranch resort in the Texas Hill Country** available for private group rentals. If you have a family gathering or retreat in mind, this place is spectacular. It comes with **its own pistol/rifle range and skeet shooting set-up** on-site【36†L105-L113】 (guests bring firearms and clays, and the ranch charges a one-time fee for range use【30†L65-L73】). Privacy is top-tier (only your group is on-site, aside from maybe some ranch staff for support). The vibe at Selah Springs Ranch is **rustic luxury and natural beauty**. ## Gear Up: What to Bring 

To make the most of your fire-making and shooting outing, don't forget these essentials:

- **Fire-Making Gear:** Pack a reliable lighter or waterproof matches, along with fire starters (dry tinder, newspaper or commercial fire starter blocks) to get your campfire going. Bring **firewood or charcoal** if the location doesn’t allow collecting wood on-site. A small shovel is handy for clearing a fire pit area and fully extinguishing the fire afterwards. **Water or a fire extinguisher** should be on hand for safety. Camping chairs or a blanket are great for getting cozy around the fire, and of course marshmallows, hot dogs, or your camp snacks of choice for that full campfire experience!

- **Shooting Gear:** Bring your **firearms and plenty of ammunition** suited to the activity (e.g. shotguns and clays for LBJ Grasslands, rifles/pistols for other spots). **Targets** are a must – paper targets, reactive targets, or clay pigeons depending on the location and rules. Many public lands require you to *clean up your targets*, so avoid using glass or junk that can't be carried out. A target stand or tape/stapler for paper targets will make life easier. **Hearing and eye protection** for all shooters (and spectators) is non-negotiable – safety first. It’s also wise to bring a basic **first aid kit** for any scrapes or minor injuries, plus a toolkit or cleaning kit in case a firearm needs a quick fix or jam is clearing. If you’re heading somewhere remote like Cross Bar, consider a GPS or offline maps, and let someone know your plans.

- **Other Handy Items:** For comfort and convenience, pack weather-appropriate clothing (hat, sunscreen for day; jacket for cool nights). A good **flashlight or headlamp** will help you manage camp chores or target setup after dark. If camping overnight, bring your tent, sleeping gear, and plenty of water and food. A cooler with drinks is great for both hydration and post-shooting refreshments (nothing like a cold one by the campfire – just save it for after the firearms are put away). And wherever you go, **bring trash bags** to leave the site cleaner than you found it.

Finally, always **double-check the specific rules** of your chosen spot before you head out. Fire restrictions can change with the weather, and private ranches may have their own guidelines or waiver forms for shooting. Once you're all set, you can look forward to a thrilling day of target practice capped off by a warm, camaraderie-building campfire night. Enjoy your adventure, and stay safe out there! 🔥🎯
