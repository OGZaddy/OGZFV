If you want to do a fresh install, hit No." indicates that the system detects a previous upgrade attempt. This situation often arises when the installation media remains connected during a system restart, causing the computer to boot from the installation media instead of the internal drive.

**To proceed with a clean installation:**

1. **Respond to the Prompt:**
   - When you see the prompt, choose "No" to initiate a fresh installation.

2. **Configure Boot Sequence:**
   - Access the BIOS or UEFI settings on your computer.
   - Adjust the boot order to prioritize the internal hard drive over external devices.
   - Save the changes and exit the BIOS/UEFI settings.

3. **Remove Installation Media:**
   - After the initial setup phase completes and the system restarts, promptly remove the USB installation media.

4. **Continue Installation:**
   - Allow the computer to boot from the internal drive to proceed with the Windows installation.

This approach ensures that the installation process continues without repeatedly booting from the installation media, thereby avoiding the recurring prompt. citeturn0search0

It looks like your screen is stuck on a blank or faded display after attempting to reinstall Windows. If your system is unresponsive, try these troubleshooting steps:

### **1. Check if the System is Active**
- See if your keyboard lights (Caps Lock, Num Lock) respond when pressed.
- If you have an HDD/SSD activity LED on your PC case, check if it's blinking.

### **2. Force Restart the System**
- Hold the **power button** for 10 seconds until the PC shuts off.
- Wait a few seconds, then power it back on.

### **3. Boot into Safe Mode**
- Turn the PC on and **force shut down** (hold power button) **three times** to trigger Windows Recovery.
- On the recovery screen, go to **Advanced Options > Startup Settings > Restart > Enable Safe Mode**.
- If Safe Mode works, there may be a driver issue.

### **4. Try Booting from the USB Again**
- Insert the Windows installation USB.
- Restart and boot into the USB drive.
- Choose **Repair Your Computer** instead of installing Windows.
- Go to **Troubleshoot > Advanced Options > Startup Repair**.

### **5. Check Your Monitor & Cables**
- If using multiple monitors, disconnect all but one.
- Try a different HDMI/DisplayPort cable or different port.
- If possible, connect your monitor to another computer to rule out a monitor issue.

### **6. Reset BIOS (if needed)**
- Turn off the PC and unplug it.
- Remove the **CMOS battery** (small round battery on the motherboard) for 5 minutes.
- Put it back in and restart.

If your system still isn't responding, let me know what happens when you try these steps!
