Your Norfolk Island Pine appears to be suffering from environmental stress, likely due to a combination of the following factors:

1. **Cold Damage** – The browning and curling of the needles, especially on the upper portions, suggest that the tree has been exposed to freezing temperatures or frost. Norfolk pines are tropical and do not tolerate cold well.

2. **Drought Stress** – If the tree has not been watered consistently, it can suffer from dehydration. Browning needles are a common sign of insufficient moisture.

3. **Sunburn or Excessive Heat** – If exposed to prolonged direct sunlight, especially in hot climates, the foliage may scorch, leading to browning.

4. **Root Issues** – If the tree is in a pot or confined space, root-bound conditions or poor drainage could lead to nutrient deficiencies, affecting needle health.

5. **Overwatering or Poor Drainage** – If the soil is too wet for extended periods, root rot can develop, causing the tree to deteriorate.

### How to Fix It:
- **Watering**: Ensure the soil is moist but not soggy. Water deeply when the top 1-2 inches of soil dry out.
- **Protection from Cold**: If possible, cover the tree during cold snaps or move it to a sheltered location.
- **Improve Drainage**: If it's in a pot, ensure proper drainage holes. If in the ground, avoid planting in a low-lying, waterlogged area.
- **Fertilization**: Use a balanced, slow-release fertilizer during the growing season (spring-summer) to encourage healthy growth.
- **Trimming**: Prune away dead or severely damaged branches to allow new growth.
