---

### 🔩 **Cut-Off Wheel** (General Term)
- **Purpose:** Cuts **anything**, depending on the material it's designed for.
- **Material:** Can be for **metal**, **plastic**, **wood**, or **multi-purpose**.
- **Construction:** Usually a **thin disc**, designed for **straight cuts only** (no grinding or sanding).
- **Variants:** 
  - Metal cut-off
  - Wood/plastic cut-off
  - Diamond or carbide edge (for tougher materials)

So basically, “cut-off wheel” just means the **style** (thin disc for cutting), and then the **material rating** tells you what it’s made to cut.

---

### ⚙️ **Metal Cutting Wheel**
- A **specific type of cut-off wheel**.
- Made with **abrasive grit** (usually aluminum oxide) for **slicing through metal**.
- Reinforced to handle higher friction and heat when cutting metal.
- **Stronger, thicker** than a wood/plastic version.

---

### Key Differences at a Glance:

| Feature | Cut-Off Wheel (Generic) | Metal Cutting Wheel |
|--------|--------------------------|----------------------|
| Style | Thin cutting disc | Same |
| Cuts | Depends on type (metal, wood, etc.) | Specifically metal |
| Durability | Varies | Built for hard material (heat/friction resistant) |
| Labeling | "Cut-off" is generic | Will specifically say "metal" on it |

---

### TL;DR:
**All metal cutting wheels are cut-off wheels**, but **not all cut-off wheels are meant for metal**.  
You want one that says **“Metal”** if you're cutting locks, bolts, or any steel hardware.
