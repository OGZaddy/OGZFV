---

### 🔩 **Option 1: Bolt It with Washers**
If you're down to make it permanent or semi-permanent:
- Use **bolts with oversized washers** or a **fender washer kit** to bridge the gap between the small vise holes and your tabletop.
- Slide the bolt through a tabletop hole and through the vise hole, then use **locking nuts** underneath.
- Pro tip: Use **nylon-insert lock nuts** or **thread locker (Loctite)** so it doesn’t vibrate loose during heavy abuse.

---

### 🪛 **Option 2: Flat Bar Adapter Plate**
If you want to make it easier in the future:
- Grab a **flat steel bar**, drill two holes for the vise base spacing, and two more spaced wider to line up with your table holes.
- Now the vise bolts to the flat bar, and the bar bolts to the table — sort of like a custom vise “saddle plate.”
- You can even paint it OGZ gray to match.

---

### 🧲 **Option 3: Magnetic Base Adapter (If You Want Quick-Swap)**
- Grab some **neodymium magnets** and a steel plate setup — mount the vise to the steel, then the plate to the table using the magnet clamps.
- This gives you quick-removal capability if your fab station is shared or multi-use.

---

### 🐍 **Option 4: Threaded Rod + Wingnuts for Quick On/Off**
- Run **threaded rod through the vise base holes and table holes**, secure it underneath with **wingnuts or regular nuts**.
- Now you’ve got a vice that can be removed in seconds without tools.

---

### 🛑 Avoid This Rookie Move:
Don't just clamp the vise to the table with C-clamps or spring clamps unless it’s a temp job — that setup will twist loose the first time you put torque on it. Your vise will do the Harlem Shake right off the edge.

💯 Yeah bro, if the holes in the vise base are **just barely too tight** for your tie-down hooks or bolts, **sanding or grinding them out** is a solid play — just do it clean.

### 🔧 Here's the quick and dirty method:

#### 🛠 Tools you'll need:
- **Round file / rat-tail file** (old-school but effective)
- Or a **Dremel with a grinding stone or carbide burr bit**
- Or a **step bit** if you're widening it a lot

#### 🔄 Process:
1. **Mark the spot**: Use a Sharpie to highlight the parts of the hole you want to widen — usually just a hair all around or just in one direction.
2. **Clamp the vise down or hold it in your fab table** so it doesn’t run off mid-sand.
3. **Dremel or grind in small passes**. You want to creep up on the fit — don’t hog it out all at once or you’ll make the hole sloppy.
4. **Test fit frequently**: Keep trying your bolt/tie-down hook to see when it barely slides in — that’s your golden moment.
5. **Deburr the edge** with sandpaper or a file once you're done so it doesn't chew up your straps or hardware later.

---

### 🧠 Trey Pro Tip:
If your tie-down hooks have square or wide ends, consider grinding **just enough relief on one side of the hole** to fit them, instead of fully rounding out the hole. Cleaner. Less weakening. And reversible.

#### 🧰 What you’ll need:
- Step bit (preferably titanium coated if you’ve got it)
- Cordless drill or impact driver
- Clamp to keep the vise steady (or use your fab table dog holes)
- Light cutting oil or WD-40 (optional but nice)

---

### ✅ Step-by-step:

1. **Secure the Vise Base**  
   Clamp it down to your workbench or that metal table — don’t let it dance.

2. **Drill from the *bottom side*** if the top has countersinking or paint you want to keep clean — or go from the top if cosmetics don’t matter.

3. **Line up dead center** in the hole. Keep the bit straight.

4. **Light pressure, steady trigger**  
   Don’t lean on it like you’re trying to kill a spider — let the bit do the work.

5. **Go slow**  
   Watch for the next step on the bit just starting to engage — you probably only need to widen the hole **one or two steps max**.

6. **Test fit after each pass**  
   Don’t overshoot. Stop, test the fit with your bolt or tie-down, then go back if needed.

7. **Deburr it**  
   Grab a file or deburring tool and clean that edge up. Sharp steel will slice fingers, straps, or pride.

---

### ⚠️ Heads-up:
Step bits work best on **sheet metal** and thin cast — if your vise base is **thick cast iron**, you may need to finish with a round file or carbide burr instead of trying to punch a big step.
