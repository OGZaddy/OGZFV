If your **subs cut off when you turn the volume past halfway**, it's almost always **one of these 4 problems** — and one of them is probably frying something 🔥

---

## 💥 Top Causes for Subs Cutting Out at High Volume:

### 1. 🔥 **Amp is going into protection mode**
**Why:** You're pushing more power than it can handle — overheating, clipping, or triggering low impedance shutdown.

**Fix:**
- Check for a little red/green light labeled "PROTECT" on the amp when it cuts
- Make sure the **gain isn't maxed out** (common mistake)
- Add a fan if it’s overheating

---

### 2. ⚡ **Voltage drop (electrical system can't keep up)**
**Why:** Your alternator or battery is sagging when the amp tries to pull a ton of current at high volume.

**Fix:**
- Check voltage at the amp with a multimeter at high volume (shouldn’t drop below 11.5V)
- Consider a **capacitor** or **upgraded battery/alternator**
- **Big 3 upgrade** on your wiring (ground, alt to batt, batt to chassis)

---

### 3. 🧠 **Wiring impedance mismatch or bad ground**
**Why:** If your subs are wired to a **too-low impedance** (e.g. 1 ohm on a 2-ohm-stable amp), it’ll shut down to protect itself.

**Fix:**
- Check your wiring configuration: dual 4-ohm subs? You might be wired to 1 ohm by accident.
- Double-check grounds: **clean paint off** grounding point, make sure wire is thick enough (8 AWG minimum, 4 AWG ideal)

---

### 4. 💀 **Clipping or gain too high**
**Why:** Cranking the gain is not how you make it louder — it causes distortion that can kill subs and force protection mode.

**Fix:**
- Back the gain down to 50–70% and re-tune
- Use a tone + voltmeter or an oscilloscope if you wanna tune it right

---

## ⚡ Real Talk Diagnostic Flow:

1. 🔌 Does the **amp shut off**, or **subs go silent but amp stays on**?
   - Amp off = protect mode, overheating, voltage
   - Amp on = maybe RCA issue or internal damage

2. 💡 Does your amp flash a **protect light** when it cuts out?

3. 🔋 Does your **headlights dim** when it happens? (Voltage sag)

## ⚡ What a Second Ground Can Do:
- Help **stabilize voltage under heavy bass hits**
- Reduce **resistance and EMI noise**
- Give amps a **direct return path** when chassis paint/coatings interfere
- Act as a failover if your first ground is *meh*

## 🔧 How to Do It Right:
1. **Use 0 or 4 gauge** to match your power wire
2. Ground to a **clean, bare-metal point on the frame or unibody**

   - Tighten with a bolt + star washer or lock washer
3. Keep ground cable **under 18 inches**
4. If using a distro block, ground **each amp individually**, or to a shared block with fat wire to chassis

- **Battery (-) ➝ Chassis**
- **Chassis ➝ Engine block**
