**Emphasizing the concept of two officers working as a team.** Shortly after our program was implemented, the Columbine High School attack shifted law enforcement priorities, marking the beginning of active-shooter-response strategies. Team-tactics training was expanded to squads of four to five officers, often armed with carbines instead of just sidearms.

The power of two or more armed individuals working together is remarkable. While many readers of this magazine are solo protectors of themselves and their families, an increasing number of couples now carry concealed weapons. By learning to operate as a team, a pair of armed citizens can significantly enhance their ability to survive a lethal encounter, whether at home or on today's increasingly dangerous streets.

### **TWO BASIC MOVEMENTS**
You should learn and regularly practice two basic movements: **“movement to contact”** and **“bounding overwatch.”**

**Movement to contact** involves a pair of armed individuals moving steadily toward a specific location at a steady, controlled pace. While there may not be an immediate threat, one could arise without much warning. With just two people on your team, your roles are clear: one of you acts as point and the other as rearguard. This means that when you're moving through an unsecured area, you must be prepared to handle threats from any direction – at full 360 degrees. 

Do not allow yourself to become too focused on your forward direction to the point that you neglect the need for one of you to watch your backs in case of a surprise attack. To move in this manner, you must train, practice, and execute clear communication to stay together.

The second technique, **bounding overwatch**, divides your team by assigning each individual a specific task. This method is most effective when you are reasonably certain that an enemy is ahead of you and the area behind you is secure.

Start by securing a solid position, ideally with adequate cover, from which to serve as the overwatch. From this position, you can monitor the downrange area and are prepared to engage any threats that may emerge. Meanwhile, your partner advances, leapfrogging ahead to gain ground to find his or her own protected position to assume the overwatch role. Once your partner is in position and ready to cover any downrange threats, you can bound ahead to the next secure location.

This technique ensures constant downrange protection, significantly enhancing the safety of the moving team member. While bounding overwatch is generally a slow process, it is an effective method for navigating potentially hostile environments.

### **INSIDE YOUR HOME**
Either of these tactical maneuvers can be effective inside a home as well as in larger, more open environments. As an armed individual, you should almost always be moving toward a goal of safety. If you must confront an intruder, using these maneuvers will ensure you are positioned to maintain as much control of the situation as possible. 

The movement-to-contact technique can be effective when you’re responding to the sound of a breach or when you’re alerted by an alarm system or a dog. Your team would move toward the threat location while ensuring the remaining family members behind you. Those family members, even small children, should be trained to seek a position of safety, ideally one that makes them difficult to locate and, if possible, provides cover that can resist bullets.

As your armed team approaches the expected threat location, you should transition to bounding overwatch to provide constant downrange coverage if necessary. However, using covering overwatch inside a home built with narrow hallways often means maintaining one-and-done safety protocols, as one of you must keep your weapon in line for the fight.

### **WHILE IN PUBLIC**
Now let's consider a larger, more open environment, such as a mall. If an attacker opens fire in the crowd, your best option for you and your partner will likely be to exit the area as quickly as possible to seek safety. Using movement-to-contact technique is the most effective way to move through the crowd while maintaining 360-degree situational awareness.

Leading other unarmed individuals to safety is also crucial. Issuing commands, such as **“stay low”** or **“follow me”**, could encourage a group of innocent bystanders to remain in line with you, using your armed movement to meet the bystanders and guide them to safety.

If an unexpected threat appears ahead of you, you should focus your attention forward, seeking cover positions whenever possible. While the best option is to remain in hiding, many are far safer with armed protectors between them and the threat than they would otherwise be.

### **TRAINING AND EXPERIENCE**
Team tactics is an advanced skill set and should be attempted only by two individuals who are already well-versed in marksmanship and scenario-based training. Seek out competent instructors who can guide you through these movement techniques. Also, make sure that you are breaking contact to retreat from an overwhelming threat when necessary.

Training with a military or law enforcement background in such training is preferred. However, it’s crucial that such training be tailored to your needs at the skill level. A former Navy SEAL or Delta operator, for example, must scale down the advanced methods of training for a modestly trained individual.
