# Excessive Spatter Causes

Why am I getting so much spatter with the flux

**Assistant** (2025-03-16T07:33:26.681557):
Excessive spatter when using flux-cored arc welding (FCAW) can be caused by several factors. Here are some common reasons and how to fix them:

### **1. Incorrect Voltage or Amperage**
- **Too high voltage**: Can cause excessive arc instability and spatter.
- **Too low voltage**: Leads to poor arc performance and excess spatter.
- **Fix**: Check the recommended settings for your wire size and material thickness. Adjust voltage and amperage accordingly.

### **2. Wrong Polarity**
- FCAW typically requires **DCEN (Direct Current Electrode Negative)** for self-shielded flux-cored wire.
- **Fix**: Ensure your machine is set to the correct polarity.

### **3. Poor Stickout Control**
- **Too much stickout (over ½ inch)**: Reduces arc stability and increases spatter.
- **Too little stickout**: Can cause overheating and inconsistent penetration.
- **Fix**: Keep the stickout around **⅜ to ½ inch**.

### **4. Incorrect Travel Speed**
- **Too slow**: Creates excess heat, leading to more spatter and potential burn-through.
- **Too fast**: Causes poor penetration and an unstable arc.
- **Fix**: Maintain a steady travel speed that allows proper fusion.

### **5. Wire Feed Speed Issues**
- **Too fast**: Wire stubs into the workpiece, causing excessive spatter.
- **Too slow**: Leads to arc instability.
- **Fix**: Adjust the wire feed speed to match your voltage settings.

### **6. Contaminated Base Metal**
- Rust, oil, paint, or dirt on the workpiece can lead to increased spatter.
- **Fix**: Always clean the metal thoroughly before welding.

### **7. Holding the Gun at the Wrong Angle**
- **Too steep (more than 15°-20°)**: Causes the arc to become unstable.
- **Fix**: Use a slight drag angle (~10°-15°) for better control.

### **8. Low-Quality Wire**
- Cheap or old flux-cored wire can lead to excessive spatter.
- **Fix**: Use high-quality wire and store it in a dry place to prevent moisture absorption.

Good question! The **10-15° angle** refers to the angle between the welding gun and the workpiece, measured from a **90° perpendicular** (straight down) position to the surface you’re welding on.

### **For Flat and Horizontal Welding:**
- Hold the gun at **a slight drag (pull) angle** of about **10-15°** from perpendicular (90°) to the workpiece.
- This means you’re tilting the gun slightly **backward** in the direction you’re welding.

### **For Vertical Welding:**
- **Vertical Up:** Use a **5-10° push angle** (pointing slightly upward).
- **Vertical Down:** Use a **slight drag angle** (pointing slightly downward).

### **For Overhead Welding:**
- Keep the gun at **a 90° perpendicular angle** to the surface to control the puddle and avoid excess spatter.

**Key Rule:** Always drag (pull) when using self-shielded flux-cored wire (FCAW-S) unless specified otherwise.
